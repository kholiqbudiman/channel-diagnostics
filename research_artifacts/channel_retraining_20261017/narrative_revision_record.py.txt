\subsection{Local masking relevance}
For example $i$ and block channel $h$, let $u_{ijh}$ be the spatial sum of activation times unsmoothed cross-entropy gradient. The signed Taylor slope is $s_j=-\mathbb E_{\rm class}\sum_hu_{ijh}$. Under a uniformly sampled fixed-size mask removing fraction $f$, the first-order expected loss change is $-f\mathbb E_{\rm class}\sum_hu_{ijh}=fs_j$. This uses the expected sum of sampled channel contributions; it does not predict the exact three finite sampled masks. The observed target is their mean class-balanced loss increase for each block. Evaluation mode keeps parameters and BN statistics fixed.

Taylor finite-mask MAE improves on zero prediction in 9/9 WaRP-C and 8/9 TrashNet contexts, but in 0/9 DTD contexts. Mean five-block Spearman correlations are 0.633, 0.122, and 0.322. The five blocks belong to one fitted model in each context, and shared image pools limit independence. No validation is claimed by comparing a mask-derived slope to the very masking responses defining it. The Taylor/mask comparison instead uses a gradient-derived quantity and measured finite removal responses. No threshold, slope, or policy is fitted using this check.

The retraining target is class-balanced loss improvement over the Base result on the corresponding probe/evaluation subset. It differs from finite masking loss increase on the diagnostic subset. \subsection{Bidirectional closure and fixed-family projection}
Let $g_+$ and $g_-$ be retrained gains under growth and shrinkage along one width, and $l_+>0,l_-<0$ their log-width displacements. A zero-anchored single slope requires $g_+/l_+=g_-/l_-$. Same-sign nonzero gains violate this restriction. A magnitude-aware closure is
\[
 C=\frac{l_+g_- - l_-g_+}{l_+-l_-}.
\]
It is the zero-displacement intercept of the line through the two observed nonreference responses; the known Base gain is zero. It is descriptive and includes training/selection variation. Mean absolute F1 closures are 2.272/2.526 pp for WaRP-C $c/d$, 2.774/1.560 for TrashNet, and 1.862/1.127 for DTD. All positive/negative pairs and zero cases are retained in the context CSV. Across tasks there are 32 same-sign pairs out of 54 axis--context measurements, not 54 independent training contexts.

Within each context, $X$ contains all six known log-width displacement pairs. Its two columns have full rank. The outcome-specific oracle $\mathbf b^*=X^+\mathbf g$ minimizes squared residual on these same six outcomes. Orthogonality gives $X^T(\mathbf g-X\mathbf b^*)=0$ and the exact squared-error identity reported in the main text. The residual is the best attainable approximation error on this observed vector within the specified zero-anchored linear family. It is not an irreducible population error, a training-noise correction, a causal variance component, or a newly validated estimator. Joint-resource matching applies to Base/joint decision candidates; the separate controls intentionally need not satisfy the budget band.
